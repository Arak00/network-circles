# Hypotheses, written before the research

Written 2026-11-06 16:18 CET, before any desk research for this round. Each persona from the earlier brainstorm is restated as the moment that makes someone reach for their network, the job they need done, and a guess at what they do today. Every prediction comes with the evidence that would kill it. Verdicts are in `research/findings.md`; this file is not edited after the research starts.

| ID | Trigger moment | Job to be done | Guessed workaround today |
|----|----------------|----------------|--------------------------|
| H1 | Lost a job, or decided to leave one | Find people I already know who can introduce me at the companies I'm targeting | LinkedIn people search filtered by company and "2nd degree", a spreadsheet of target companies |
| H2 | Raising money or starting to sell (founder, freelancer) | Find who in my network can open a door to an investor or a customer | Export connections to CSV, enrich them in a spreadsheet or Clay, or pay for Sales Navigator |
| H3 | Back from a conference or meetup | Follow up with the people I met before I forget who they were | Connect on LinkedIn on the spot, notes on the phone, a personal CRM (Folk, Dex) |
| H4 | Changed jobs, or noticed I've lost touch | Keep in touch with former colleagues and friends on a rhythm | Personal CRM reminders (Dex, Folk, Clay), calendar reminders, birthday notifications |
| H5 | Opened a role to fill (recruiter, hiring manager) | Find candidates with a skill among people I already know | LinkedIn Recruiter or ordinary search, asking the team for referrals |
| H6 | Trying to grow an audience (creator) | Understand who reads and reacts to my posts | LinkedIn's own post analytics, creator tools (Shield, Taplio) |
| H7 | Student or first job | Find alumni and mentors who can help | LinkedIn's alumni tool, the university careers office |
| H8 | Curiosity, or a privacy concern | See what my network looks like, or what LinkedIn holds about me | Download the data archive and open the CSV, or a one-off visualisation |

## Predictions

H0 (all personas). A picture of your network by itself is a one-off curiosity, not something people come back to. Killed if sources show people returning to a network visualisation repeatedly, or asking for one as an ongoing tool, rather than viewing it once.

H1. For job seekers, the most frequent network job is getting a warm introduction at a specific company, and the complaint is that LinkedIn search shows 2nd-degree people without telling you who actually knows them well. Killed if job seekers mostly talk about job listings, applications or recruiters and rarely about introductions through people they know.

H2. Founders and salespeople who use their own network export it to a spreadsheet or Clay to enrich and filter it; the pain is that the export is thin (no emails for most people, no industry, no seniority). Killed if the evidence shows they don't use their own first-degree network for this, or that Sales Navigator alone satisfies them.

H3. After events the pain is remembering who you met and following up, not measuring whether the event grew your network. Killed if organisers or attendees are found asking how many connections or what kind of people an event brought them.

H4. Keeping in touch is the most often repeated job of all, it is what personal CRMs sell, and the pain is forgetting to reach out rather than not knowing who is in your network. Killed if personal-CRM reviews mostly praise or ask for network mapping or discovery rather than reminders and notes.

H5. Recruiters will not use a map of their own first-degree network, because LinkedIn Recruiter already searches all of LinkedIn. Killed if recruiters are found asking to export or map their own connections for sourcing.

H6. Creators want audience analytics (who reacts, who follows), which the export does not contain, so this tool can't serve them. Killed if creators are found asking for the structure of their connection network rather than their audience.

H7. Students are served well enough by LinkedIn's alumni tool, and their networks are too small for circles to mean much. Killed if students are found asking for ways to map or understand their network beyond alumni search.

H8. Visualising your own network is a one-time curiosity; InMaps was retired because people didn't come back. Killed if coverage of the InMaps shutdown gives a different reason (cost, strategy, data access) and users missed it as a regular tool.
